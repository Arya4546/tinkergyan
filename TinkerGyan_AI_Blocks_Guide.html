TinkerGyan AI Studio
Comprehensive Step-by-Step Guide

Welcome! This guide is designed to help students, educators, and creators understand exactly how to build smart, interactive projects using the TinkerGyan AI Blocks. By the end of this guide, you will know how to make your projects see objects, hear words, recognize emotions, and even track your fingers in the air.

Understanding the Types of AI Blocks

Before we begin, it's important to understand the three main types of blocks you will use:

  • Command Block (Action Blocks): These are standard rectangular blocks (like turn AI vision [ON]). They tell the computer to do something immediately.
  • Hat Block (Event Listeners): These have a curved top (like when I hear [zero]). They sit by themselves on the canvas. Any blocks you attach underneath them will automatically trigger whenever the AI detects that specific event.
  • Reporter Block (Data Blocks): These have rounded edges (like (AI prediction)). They don't do anything on their own; instead, they hold information (like text or numbers). You drop them inside other blocks to use that data.

CRITICAL RULE: Always Turn the AI On First!

The system needs your permission to access the computer's camera and microphone. If you do not explicitly turn the AI feature ON, your other AI blocks will not do anything. You should always put the turn [Feature] ON block directly underneath your when ⚑ clicked block.

1. 📷 Custom AI Vision (Image Classification)

This feature allows you to train the computer to recognize your own custom objects. For example, you can teach it to tell the difference between an Apple and a Banana using your webcam.

Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. 1Train your model: First, go to the AI Trainer Modal in the interface. Create two classes (e.g., "Apple" and "Banana"). Use your webcam to take several pictures of an apple for the first class, and pictures of a banana for the second class. Click Train.
  2. 2Turn on the Vision: Go back to the block editor. Drag out a when ⚑ clicked block. Attach a turn AI vision [ON] block under it. This tells the webcam to turn on and start looking at the real world.
  3. 3Create a reaction: Drag out the hat block when AI sees [Apple]. Attach a block underneath it saying say [It's an Apple!] for (2) seconds.
  4. 4Run the code: Click the Green Flag. The webcam will appear faintly behind your Stage. Hold up an apple to the camera. The Sprite will instantly say "It's an Apple!".

Example Code Structure:

// Block Group 1: Start the Engine
when ⚑ clicked
turn AI vision [ON]

// Block Group 2: Define what happens when it sees an Apple
when AI sees [Apple]
say [It's a healthy Apple!] for (2) seconds

// Block Group 3: Define what happens when it sees a Banana
when AI sees [Banana]
say [It's a Banana!] for (2) seconds

2. 🎤 Speech Commands (Word Recognition)

This feature allows your project to listen for 20 specific, built-in keywords: zero to nine, up, down, left, right, yes, no, go, stop, on, off. It's perfect for making voice-controlled games!

Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. 1Turn on the Microphone: Under when ⚑ clicked, attach the turn microphone [ON] block.
  2. 2Set up the Event: Drag out the when I hear [up] Hat block.
  3. 3Add an Action: Under the Hat block, attach a change y by (10) block. Now, every time you say the word "up" out loud, your character will jump up the screen!
  4. 4Add more directions: Duplicate the event, change the dropdown to down, and attach change y by (-10). Now you can navigate using your voice.

Example Code Structure:

when ⚑ clicked
turn microphone [ON]

when I hear [up]
change y by (10)

when I hear [down]
change y by (-10)

3. 😊 Face & Emotion Detection

This AI feature scans the webcam feed to detect human faces and determine their emotional expression (Happy, Sad, Angry, Surprised, Disgusted, Fearful, or Neutral).

Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. 1Turn on Emotion AI: Under when ⚑ clicked, attach turn emotion detection [ON].
  2. 2Detect Smiles: Drag out the when AI detects [happy 😊] face block. Attach say [You look very happy!] for 2 seconds under it.
  3. 3Track the Face (Advanced): You can make a sprite follow your nose! Use a forever loop. Inside it, put a go to x: () y: () block. Inside the X and Y slots, drop the (face [x] position) and (face [y] position) Reporter blocks.

Example Code Structure:

when ⚑ clicked
turn emotion detection [ON]

when AI detects [surprised 😮] face
change [size] by (10)
say [Whoa! You look shocked!] for (2) seconds

4. ✋ Hand & Finger Tracking

This powerful feature maps the exact coordinates of your hand and fingers, and can recognize specific hand gestures like "Thumbs Up" or "Pinching".

Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. 1Turn on Hand Tracking: Under when ⚑ clicked, attach turn hand tracking [ON].
  2. 2Gesture Control: Drag out the when hand is [thumbs up 👍] block. Attach say [Good job!] underneath it.
  3. 3Virtual Drawing (Advanced): Create a new sprite that looks like a pencil. Write a script: forever -> go to x: (x position of [index finger tip]) y: (y position of [index finger tip]). Your pencil sprite will now perfectly track the tip of your index finger in real time!

5. 🗣️ Speech-to-Text & Text-to-Speech

Unlike simple Speech Commands (which only listen for 20 words), Speech-to-Text can listen to entire conversational sentences. Text-to-Speech allows the computer to talk back to you in a human voice.

Step-by-Step Tutorial: Building a Virtual Assistant

  1. 1Start listening: Under when ⚑ clicked, attach start listening for speech.
  2. 2Listen for a phrase: Use the when speech contains [phrase] Hat block. Type "hello" into the phrase box.
  3. 3Make the computer speak: Underneath the Hat block, attach the say aloud [Text] in [Language] block. Type "Hello there, how can I assist you?" into the text box and select "English".

Example Code Structure:

when ⚑ clicked
start listening for speech

when speech contains "hello"
say aloud "Hello there, how can I assist you?" in [English]

when speech contains "weather"
say aloud "It looks like a beautiful day outside!" in [English]

6. 📝 Text Classification (Custom NLP)

Train the AI to read a sentence and categorize it. For instance, you could train it to read a movie review and decide if it is "Positive" or "Negative".

Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. 1Train the Model: In the AI Studio, type several positive sentences into Class 1 (name it "Positive") and several negative sentences into Class 2 (name it "Negative"). Click Train.
  2. 2Ask for Input: Under when ⚑ clicked, use the standard Scratch block ask [How are you feeling?] and wait.
  3. 3Classify the Answer: Use an if <> then block. Check if (classify text (answer)) = "Positive". If it is, make the sprite jump for joy!

7. 🎵 AI Music Generation

Using Google's Magenta neural network, the computer can listen to a few notes you provide, and dynamically generate a brand new song that matches your style!

Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. 1Start the Music: Grab the 🎸 play AI melody from base notes block.
  2. 2Provide base notes: Provide an array of numbers representing MIDI notes (e.g., [60, 62, 64]).
  3. 3Set Creativity: Set the "generate steps" to 20 (how many new notes to make) and the "creativity" to 1.5. A higher creativity means the AI will take more risks with the melody!
